5 reports read as a one-day move; filed under parser by tenant.

Parser: the shared parser reads month first although the tenant writes day first. For example, Harbour Dental’s report R-41200 says 7 April became 4 July; R-41201 corrects the direction of its own report. Use the tenant’s locale, not the reporter’s browser locale. Validate the parser with ambiguous dates in both directions and reject impossible dates.

Hint: booking-v3 displays MM/DD/YYYY while its form reads dates in the day-first tenant’s order. This affects form reports for Alder Physio, Copper Studio and Pale Harbour. Align the visible hint with the form’s parsing order and test submission through the actual form. R-41230 and R-41241 illustrate this group. App and email reports from these same tenants still belong to the parser group under the supplied classifier.

The one-day-move notes identify reports whose wording does not establish a month/day swap. They remain included in the parser total under the supplied classifier, not extra reports or a proven third cause. Preserve their bodies and investigate actual timestamps, time zones and reproduction steps before concluding what caused them. What the queue shows

How to triage now

Look up the tenant’s locale and form version, read the body and identify the channel. Check the cause and merge only reports with the same supported cause. Keep unrelated problems separate even when their subjects match. Missing tenant data or uncertain evidence means investigate, not automatic merge. Matching classifier output is a triage rule, not proof that every report has the same underlying defect.
